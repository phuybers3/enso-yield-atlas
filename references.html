Data sources and references

Sources documented for release 2026-10-19. We combine reported agricultural statistics from censuses, surveys and administrative records with crop calendars, weather observations and satellite products. Agency extensions retain attribution to both the original compilation and the updating provider.

Dataset citations below identify outstanding attribution and version checks explicitly. See also detailed release documentation and acknowledgements.

Agricultural records

  1. Lee, D., Anderson, W., Chen, X. et al. (2025). HarvestStat Africa – Harmonized Subnational Crop Statistics for Sub-Saharan Africa. Scientific Data 12, 690.

    Dataset: doi:10.5061/dryad.vq83bk42w; atlas register identifies version 1.2.

  2. HarvestStat collaboration. HarvestStat Asia, supplied packages v0.1 and v0.2, including crop statistics and season calendars.

    Contributor-approved dataset citation and separate attribution for India’s stable reporting groups remain to be confirmed.

  3. Hultgren, A., Carleton, T., Delgado, M. et al. (2025). Impacts of climate change on global agriculture accounting for adaptation. Nature 642, 644–652.
  4. Hultgren, A. (2024). Replication data and code for Hultgren et al. 2025 “Climate Change Impacts on Global Agriculture Accounting for Adaptation”, version 1. Zenodo.

    The agency sources below extend these records on matched reporting units.

  5. USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service. Quick Stats: crop yield, production and area statistics.

    US agency extensions.

  6. Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística. Produção Agrícola Municipal, SIDRA table 1612.

    Municipal agricultural statistics.

  7. Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística. SIDRA table 839.

    First- and second-crop maize statistics.

  8. Ministerio de Agricultura, Ganadería y Pesca, Argentina. Estimaciones Agrícolas.

    Crop area, production and yield statistics.

  9. Eurostat. Crop production in EU standard humidity by NUTS 2 region, dataset apro_cpshr.
  10. Statistische Ämter des Bundes und der Länder. Regionaldatenbank Deutschland.

    Specific German table identifiers remain to be documented.

  11. Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations. FAOSTAT: Crops and livestock products, domain QCL.

    National yield, production and harvested-area records.

Crop calendars

  1. Laborte, A. G. et al. (2017). RiceAtlas, a spatial database of global rice calendars and production. Scientific Data 4, 170074.

    Dataset: doi:10.7910/DVN/JE6R2R.

  2. Companhia Nacional de Abastecimento (2022). Calendário de Plantio e Colheita de Grãos no Brasil.

    Brazilian second-crop maize planting and harvest windows.

  3. FAO Global Information and Early Warning System. Country crop calendars.
  4. Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations. Crop Calendar.
  5. Becker-Reshef, I., Barker, B., Mobley, K., Justice, C., Sanchez Galvez, A. and Sahajpal, R. (2026). GEOGLAM Crop Monitor Sub-National Crop Calendars, version 1.4. Zenodo.
  6. Dimou, M., Meroni, M. and Rembold, F. (2018). Development of a national and sub-national crop calendars data set compatible with remote sensing derived land surface phenology. European Commission Joint Research Centre, EUR 29327 EN.
  7. Jägermeyr, J., Müller, C., Minoli, S., Ray, D. and Siebert, S. (2021). GGCMI Phase 3 crop calendar. Zenodo.

    The deposit is labelled v1; the atlas register labels its calendar v1.01. Version reconciliation is pending.

  8. Huybers, P. and project contributors. Detailed local calendars and derived grain-filling windows from the Hultgren repository.

    Project-developed products; final contributor list and versioned public archive pending. Hultgren-panel median dates and HarvestStat season calendars are retained as distinct sources.

Weather and satellite observations

  1. Berkeley Earth. Daily gridded land temperature. Rohde, R. A. and Hausfather, Z. (2020). The Berkeley Earth land/ocean temperature record. Earth System Science Data 12, 3469–3479.

    Daily product documentation: berkeleyearth.org/data/; the article describes the broader temperature record.

  2. NOAA Climate Prediction Center. CPC Global Unified Temperature: daily maximum and minimum temperature, distributed by NOAA Physical Sciences Laboratory.
  3. Funk, C. et al. (2015). The climate hazards infrared precipitation with stations—a new environmental record for monitoring extremes. Scientific Data 2, 150066.

    CHIRPS version 2.0, final and preliminary products where available.

  4. Hersbach, H. et al. (2020). The ERA5 global reanalysis. Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society 146, 1999–2049.

    Precipitation outside CHIRPS coverage and supporting comparisons.

  5. NOAA Center for Satellite Applications and Research. Blended Vegetation Health Product: weekly smoothed NDVI from AVHRR and VIIRS.

    Cropland extraction contributed by Michael Foley.

ENSO, crop areas and boundaries

  1. Huang, B. et al. (2017). Extended Reconstructed Sea Surface Temperature, version 5 (ERSSTv5): upgrades, validations, and intercomparisons. Journal of Climate 30, 8179–8205.
  2. van Oldenborgh, G. J. et al. (2021). Defining El Niño indices in a warming climate. Environmental Research Letters 16, 044003.
  3. NOAA Climate Prediction Center. ENSO Diagnostic Discussion and accompanying outlook products.

    The atlas labels its input “CPC October 2026 RONI outlook”. The exact archived quantitative input citation remains to be reconciled. CPC RONI and the atlas’s relative Niño 3.4 index are distinct.

  4. Tang, F. H. M. et al. (2024). CROPGRIDS: a global geo-referenced dataset of 173 crops. Scientific Data 11, 413.

    Dataset version recorded: doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.22491997.v9.

  5. Natural Earth. Admin 0—Countries, 1:50 million scale.
  6. Subnational reporting boundaries: source-package geometries, HarvestStat/FEWS NET, FAO GAUL and Geolocet India.

    Provider-specific vintages and India attribution remain to be completed; geometry does not by itself establish stable reporting boundaries.
