Reading the El Niño Crop Watch

We compare historical crop responses, observed seasonal weather and conditional yield changes. A useful story identifies the crop, place, harvest year, growing season and observation cutoff. We describe conditional yield changes relative to trend; a statement about global food supply or household food access requires additional evidence.

A historical relationship with evidence

Open Argentine maize relationships. The country regression gives +10.5% per +1 °C of relative Niño 3.4, with an interval of +4.4% to +17.0%. The relationship survives the release’s multiple-comparison adjustment (q=0.017). Then inspect the local map and regional estimates. A positive historical relationship is not evidence that every district benefits or that the coming harvest gain is known.

When observed weather differs from ENSO expectations

Open US maize this season. Compare the ENSO and weather cards, then open the comparison over the same places. The matched unit means are +7.8% for ENSO and -2.9% for weather. Check the skill chart at the current lead and compare observed temperature and rainfall with their ENSO expectations. Describe the geographic coverage whenever citing either estimate.

When the evidence is insufficient

Open Brazilian maize this season. Most of the selected crop has not yet been planted. Weather coverage is 0.14% of production, and the weather method performs worse than trend in the corresponding historical test. The page preserves the number for inspection and labels the failed checks. A headline national loss based on that weather number would overstate the available evidence.

Spend five minutes explaining the three views and observation cutoff, five minutes on each example above, and five minutes on questions and exports. Peter and Klara could divide the examples between historical ENSO relationships and this season’s interpretation, subject to their availability. This page supplies an agenda and demonstration links; no webinar has been scheduled.

Questions to ask before quoting a result

Identify the baseline, sign and unit. Check whether the interval includes zero, whether a model passed its historical tests at this lead, and whether it is being applied beyond the fitted range. Confirm whether a national figure covers the whole country, a subset of reporting units or one season. Distinguish lower and higher ENSO scenarios from lower and higher loss percentiles.

Use the dated release bulletin for a short overview and methods and downloads for provenance. The FEWS NET regional assessment provides complementary food-security context. Any public article should retain the observation date and uncertainty next to the quoted estimate.
